# 22 - Sound: every POKEY write in the ROM, reached, understood and measured

Written 2026-11-11. Closes OV-10 of `OPEN-VERIFICATION.md` (the sounds no earlier run reached) and extends `21-start-gameover-title.md` (start tune) and `20-pacman-and-death.md` (death glide). Nothing in this chapter was settled by listening: the project owner listened once to the start tune and confirmed it, and everything else is measured.

## The census: 31 sound-register writes, 4 channels' worth of sounds

A scan of the whole ROM for every store (`STA/STX/STY` absolute, `STA abs,X/Y`) to the POKEY page (`tests/test_sound.py`, `test_census_of_pokey_stores`) finds 33 instructions: two set-up writes (`IRQEN $A453`, `SKCTL $A45D`) and **31 sound-register writes**. There is no sound code for the **extra life** (`$B487`: `INC lives`, then the life display is redrawn) or for the **board change** (`AdvanceLevel $AC75`): neither contains a POKEY store (tested), and in the live run neither produced a new sound writer or a start tune. ## Who owns channel 2 (code and measurement agree)

`Sub_AF54` (`$AF54`, called once per frame from `$A994`) decides:

- `FrightenPhase <> 0` and `zp_F2 = 0`: call the frightened sound (`Sub_AFD7`);
- `FrightenPhase = 0` and `zp_F2 = 0`: jump to the siren (`LB508`), which also sets `zp_E4 := 0`, so the frightened sound starts from its beginning next time;
- `zp_F2 <> 0` (an eaten ghost is returning): nothing here; the **eyes** sound is called from the ghost update at `$B6FF` whenever `zp_F2 <> 0` and no ghost-eaten sound runs (`zp_E5 = 0`).

So the priority on channel 2 is **eyes > frightened > siren**, and a **ghost eaten** mutes channel 2 (`$A8EE`) and takes over the registers for 34 frames: from `$A840` the main routine jumps straight to `LB049` while `zp_E5 <> 0`, so the rest of the frame (movement, collisions, the siren) does not run: the game stands still for 0.57 s. Measured over the 175 s session (frames in each situation, writers of `AUDC2`): normal: siren `$B54B` in 5,374 frames; frightened: `$B028` in 1,494, siren in 0; eyes: `$B028` in 1,728, siren in 0; ghost-eaten: no channel-2 write except `$A8EE` at the start and `$B0EA` at the end.

## The sounds, with numbers (pitch f = 63,921 / (2 x (AUDF + 1)) Hz)

- **Bonus item** (`Sub_ABC9`, `zp_F7`/`zp_F8`; 31 calls, one per frame, 0.52 s): `AUDF1` rises in steps of 5 from `$7D` to `$B4` over 12 calls (254 Hz falling to 177 Hz) with `AUDC1 = $A5`; then `$B4` for 6 more calls with `AUDC1 = $A3`; then falls in steps of 5 back to `$78` (264 Hz, `AUDC1 = $A3`); the last call writes `AUDF1 = AUDC1 = 0`. Started by `A96C/A970` (`zp_F7 := 1`, `zp_F8 := $78`) in the code that adds the item's score.
- **Ghost eaten** (`$A8EC`, `LB049`, `LB067`, `LB085`; 34 frames, 0.57 s): `AUDC2 := 0`, `zp_E7 := $84`, `zp_E6 := 2`. Each frame `zp_E6` is decremented: when it is non-zero (odd frames) `zp_E7 -= 4`, `AUDF1 = AUDF4 = zp_E7`, `AUDCTL := $84`, `AUDC1 = AUDC4 = $A4`; when it reaches 0 (every second frame) `zp_E6 := 2`, `zp_E7 -= 3`, `AUDF1 = AUDF3 = AUDF4 = zp_E7`, `AUDC1 = AUDC3 = AUDC4 = $A8`. When `zp_E7 - 4` would be `$10`, `ClearPokeyRegisters`, `AUDCTL := 0`, the ghost colour `$DC` is restored to `$0C` and the game goes on. The pitch rises from 248 Hz (`$80`) to 1,522 Hz (`$14`). `AUDCTL` bit 2 puts a **high-pass filter on channel 1 clocked by channel 3**: the waveform of the whole sweep is predicted sample by sample from the register writes in the section "The ghost-eaten sweep predicted" below.
- **Frightened** (`Sub_AFD7`, `zp_E1`-`zp_E4`): a triangle wobble of `AUDF2` between `$90` (220 Hz) and `$40` (492 Hz) in steps of `$10`: `$80 $70 $60 $50 $40 $50 $60 $70 $80 $90`, ten calls (167 ms) per cycle, about 6 cycles a second. `AUDC2` volume per cycle: 5 in the first, then 4, 3, 4, 3, ... (`zp_E3`: 5, then `dec` per cycle; at 3 it is set back to 5 first, so it alternates 4, 3).
- **Eyes** (`Sub_B02C`, `zp_F3`): `AUDF2` = 13, 14, ... 23, 13, 14, ... (2,283 Hz falling to 1,332 Hz, then a jump back up), `AUDC2 = $A4`; `zp_F3 := 0` whenever `zp_F2 = 0`, so each return journey starts at 13.
- **Siren**, **dot sweep**, **start tune**, **death glide**: unchanged from `20` and `21` and re-measured here (siren 3,951 + 5,185 segments, pitch within 1.6 cents).

## How it was checked (three independent layers)

1. **Register level, against the code** (`tools/analyze_sound_events.py`, `analysis/evidence-sound-events-registers-summary.txt`). Each routine is ported to Python from its disassembly and run against every write of every episode in the trace: bonus **4 of 4 episodes** equal (31 calls each); ghost eaten **14 of 14** equal (34 frames each, every register and value); frightened **1,500 calls in 7 episodes, 0 mismatches**; eyes **1,735 calls, 0 mismatches**. The writes of each routine happen only while its conditions hold (checked at the start of the routine and of the next, because the state can change inside a routine). Events: 7 power pellets, 14 ghosts eaten, 4 bonus items, 1 extra life (lives 2 -> 3, no sound routine involved), 1 board change (no start tune).
2. **Audio, against the registers** (`tools/analyze_tail_sound.py` on Altirra's real output, `analysis/evidence-sound-events-summary.txt`). The recording (176 s, 44.1 kHz) is aligned by the first tone of the start tune; the writes get their exact time from frame + beam position. 8,180 segments of constant register state were judged (8,156 with a pitch measurement): pitch error: segments of 40 ms or more 54 tones, mean 0.12 cents, worst 2.0; the 8,302 tones in 12-40 ms segments (sirens, sweeps) mean 0.25 cents, 95% within 0.4 (the worst, 79.6 cents, is at the 80-cent limit of the shortest windows); level of single tones 0.87-1.13 of the expected. Frightened (`$B021`), eyes (`$B042`), bonus (`$ABFE`) and the ghost-eaten sweep (`$B058`-`$B080`) are all in the table with their writer PCs. The silence between and after sounds is checked as silent (150 Hz and above, limit from the quietest possible sound: a volume-1 tone is detected at 131-1,500 Hz).
3. **Edge timing of the bonus sweep** (same tool): the sweep is too fast for a per-segment pitch measurement (each pitch lasts one frame at 177-264 Hz), so the predicted square-wave edge times from the AUDF history (a channel reloads from AUDF at each underflow, so a new AUDF takes effect when the half-cycle in progress ends) are compared with the zero crossings of the recording: **207 predicted edges per episode, median residual 0.04-0.05 ms in all three episodes where channel 1 is alone in the band; the worst single edge is 0.11 ms and 0.16 ms in two of them and 1.62 ms (one edge of 207) in the third**. The fourth episode overlaps the frightened-phase tone in the same band, where zero crossings of the mixture are not channel 1's edges, so the method does not apply and it is skipped (its registers still equal the port, and the other three prove the sweep's shape).

## Does any sound depend on the level or on how many dots are left? (OV-11, OV-12)

**What the code reads** (`tools/sound_inputs.py`, tested in `tests/test_sound.py::test_no_sound_routine_reads_the_level`). Each sound routine is decoded instruction by instruction and every location it reads is listed. **None of the eleven routines reads `Level` (`$30/$31`) or `StartLevel` (`$1B`), and none indexes a table by level.** Their inputs are their own state variables, `RTCLOK2` (the start tune's 4-frame step) and constant tables (the tune and the dot sweep). There is **one outside input**: the siren's step.

- **Siren step `zp_9C` comes from the dots eaten** (`Sub_B5C7`, `$B5D3-$B5FF`, which runs at `$A9CE`, after the siren at `$A994` in the same routine, so the siren uses the previous routine's value; it is still 0 at the very first call after a board set-up): 2 below 176 dots eaten, 3 from 176 (`$B0`), 4 from 224 (`$E0`), 5 from 240 (`$F0`) or when the high byte `zp_36` is non-zero. The siren (`zp_DE`, a triangle in `AUDF2`) moves `AUDF2` by that step each frame, so it speeds up as the board empties: step 2: `AUDF2` 38..64 (492-819 Hz), a full up-and-down in 26 frames (434 ms); step 3: 37..64, 18 frames (300 ms); step 4: 36..64, 14 frames (234 ms); step 5: 35..65 (up to 888 Hz), 12 frames (200 ms). The board set-up clears the siren state (`zp_E0`, `zp_9C`), so each new board starts again at `$28`. (`Sub_B5C7` also releases waiting ghosts at 224 and 240 dots: not sound.)
- **The level acts only on WHEN and HOW LONG a sound runs, never on the sound itself.** The routines that read `Level` are the bonus score (`$A93F`, `$A974`), the level indicator, the speed tables (`$B5A5`), the pellet (`FrightenFrames`, `$B3F7`), the end of the frightened time (`FrightenEndPhase`, `$AF71`) and the bonus item's code (`$B662`). So the frightened sound lasts as long as the level's frightened time (`14-levels.md`), the eyes sound as long as a ghost needs to get home (ghost speed), and a level with no frightened time has a one-call frightened sound.

**Live sweep** (`tools/altirra_sound_levels.cmd`, driver `tools/run_sound_levels.py`, summary `analysis/evidence-sound-levels-summary.txt`, one file per run in `analysis/sound-levels/`): the sound session of the previous section, with the dot counter in the trace, repeated in **16 configurations**: the level counter poked at `$ADFD` to 0, 1, 4, 8, 12, 13, 18, 19, 20, 40, 70, 100, 254, 255 (as `altirra_catch.cmd` does), and the real StartLevel menu with 3 and 7 OPTION presses (StartLevel 4 and 12). In each, five 900-frame cycles set the dot counter to `$50`/`$A0` (bonus item), `$B2`, `$E2`, `$F1` (siren steps 3, 4, 5) and eat a pellet and two ghosts. **All 16 traces pass `analyze_sound_events.py` (`PROBLEMS: none`)**: 514,244 frames; the start tune equals the ROM tables in 16 of 16 (64 steps each, one step per 4 frames, **also at level counters 4 and 12 reached through the real OPTION menu, and at every poked level**); the siren equals its port in 314,062 calls (steps 2 / 3 / 4 / 5: 68,676 / 31,705 / 31,877 / 181,745 calls, 0 mismatches); the frightened sound in 53,312 calls and the eyes sound in 89,887 calls (0 mismatches against the ports); 890 ghost-eaten and 116 bonus-item episodes equal their ports. Frightened sound length against the level chapter: at level 20 the median is **197 game frames** (195-198; `14-levels.md` gives 146 + (4-2) x 24 + 2 = 196); at levels 18, 19, 40 and 100 some pellets give a **one-call** frightened sound (the zero-length frightened time); at level 70 one frightened phase lasted **5,295 frames** without ending (the endless frightened level of `14-levels.md`).

**Audio at another level** (`analysis/sound-level12-recorded.wav`, level counter 12, 152 s, siren steps 2-5 all present, bonus sweeps included): `tools/analyze_tail_sound.py`: 7,488 segments judged, `PROBLEMS: none`; pitch error 0.12 cents mean on 54 long tones and 0.53 cents mean on 7,615 short ones (worst 79.4 cents, at the 80-cent limit); bonus-sweep edge timing 207 edges per episode, median residual 0.05-0.12 ms, 99-100% within 0.5 ms (`analysis/evidence-sound-level12-summary.txt`).

## The ghost-eaten sweep predicted (OV-13)

The sweep is the one sound whose waveform depends on POKEY's internal state: it sets `AUDCTL = $84`, which puts a high-pass flip-flop on channel 1 clocked by channel 3, and runs channels 1, 3 and 4 at (nearly) the same pitch, so the phases between them decide the level. Bounds were all that the register writes seemed to give (OV-13 as first written). The result is a model that needs nothing but the register writes and the boot time of the machine: the start state of the flip-flops (two bits per sweep) is derived too (point 4).

**1. The timers are exact.** Altirra's debugger command `.pokey` lists, for every channel, the output level, the CPU cycles until its next underflow and the period. From 4,091 dumps (`tools/altirra_sound_levels.cmd` with `-Complete`, fast runs) all 16,364 channel readings of "cycles until fire" are reproduced **exactly** by `tools/pokey_timers.py` from the register writes alone, starting at reset, with one global constant and without any dump as input (`python -I tools/analyze_ghost_sweep.py --validate-timers <trace>`). The rules: all underflows ("fires") of all channels lie on one 28-cycle grid (the 64 kHz divider); at a fire the output flip-flop toggles and the channel reloads with the AUDF it has **at that moment**, so the next fire is (AUDF + 1) x 28 cycles later; an AUDF write does not move the pending fire. (Tested alternatives, "the new value applies at once" and "from the last underflow", reproduce 0 to 6% of the readings.)

**2. The audio model.** Level = Altirra's mix of volume x output for channels 1, 3, 4 (pure-tone `AUDC`, channel 2 is silenced by `$A8EE`; the mixer is not a plain sum, see point 6); at every channel-3 fire a latch takes channel 1's output and the audible output of channel 1 is its output XOR the latch XOR a constant polarity. The simulated level is averaged to the recording's 44.1 kHz and compared with Altirra's real audio by normalised cross-correlation (both band-passed 100 Hz-5 kHz).

**3. Result** (`tools/analyze_ghost_sweep.py`, `analysis/evidence-ghost-sweep-*.txt`, four recordings, **87 ghost-eaten sweeps**; two of the recordings are repeats of the same deterministic run):
- the global time offset of the recording against the cycle timeline is **0.00 to +0.02 ms** (the anchor is the first fire of the start tune, which is therefore the first audible edge);
- every sweep is reproduced by the model at a correlation of **0.951 to 0.964** (median 0.958-0.959 per recording; with the exact NTSC clock, see OV-14), and the best assignment of the two start bits is clearly distinct: the next-best assignment is at least **0.28** lower (typically 0.6 against 0.95); the start bits themselves are predicted (point 4);
- the high-pass polarity bit is the **same constant (1) in 87 of 87 sweeps**; the two possible orders of an event of channel 1 and one of channel 3 at the very same cycle cannot be told apart (median 0.955 against 0.957);
- the **level**: the four possible start-bit assignments differ in RMS by a factor 1.05 only (min 1.02, max 1.07), so the level of the sweep is fixed by the register writes to within about +-3% whatever the start bits are. With a plain linear sum of the volumes the model's RMS was 1.09 to 1.17 times the recording's (median 1.12-1.16); with Altirra's real mixer chain (point 6) it is **0.95 to 1.02** (medians per recording; min 0.91, max 1.05 over the sweeps), i.e. the 12-16% excess was the mixer, not an error of the register model;
- frame by frame: the amplitude at the pitch of channels 3 and 4 swings between 2.4 and 14.8 volume units because two volume-8 channels add coherently with a changing phase; the filtered channel 1 is fitted with weight -0.97 to -0.98 against channels 3+4 (it contributes with its full volume).

**4. The start state of the flip-flops of channels 3 and 4 is derived from Altirra's renderer (OV-13a, `tools/pokey_noise.py`).** The two bits per sweep looked random for a long time: they were not the parity of any fire count, not a lagged parity, and not the debugger's `Output` field (which is a different object: for channels at `AUDF = 0` it is uncorrelated with the tick parity). The answer is in Altirra's source (`src/ATAudio/source/pokeyrenderer.cpp`, `FireTimer<>`, version 4.40, the open-source archive on virtualdub.org): the renderer keeps its own **noise flip-flop** per channel and updates it at every timer fire.
- in a **pure-tone mode** (`AUDC` bits 7-5 = 101, e.g. `$A8`) it toggles at every fire;
- in a **noise mode** (`AUDC = $00`, what the OS leaves in idle channels; and with the 5-bit gate unless `AUDC` bit 7 is set) it is *set* to the current bit of the 17-bit polynomial (9-bit when `AUDCTL` bit 7 is set) at every fire that passes the 5-bit gate (the fire is skipped when the poly5 bit is 0);
- the polynomial counters run freely from the moment the OS writes `SKCTL := 3` (leaves initialisation mode): frame 22, vpos 191, hpos 103 of every trace that logs `$D20F` = cycle **678,973**; at a fire at cycle *f* of channel *c* (0..3) the 17-bit table is read at `(131070 - c + f - T0) mod 131071`, the 9-bit at `(510 - c + f - T0) mod 511`, the 5-bit gate at `(29 - c + f - T0) mod 31` (tables generated exactly as `pokeytables.cpp`; XNOR registers, so the all-ones state never occurs);
- fires of the last 2 cycles before a register write are processed in the mode written (Altirra's audio delay `kAudioDelay` = 2).

So a channel that enters pure tone (the sweep writes `$A4`/`$A8` into channel 3/4) starts at the poly bit of **its last gated fire before the switch**, and the start constant of the model is that bit XOR the parity of all fires counted since reset XOR one fixed polarity (1, the same for both channels). Between the idle stretch and the tone the sweep sets `AUDCTL = $84`, so channel 3 (tone about 59,000 cycles after the window start, long after `AUDCTL`) samples the **9-bit** polynomial and channel 4 (tone about 29,000 cycles after the start, 9 cycles after `AUDCTL`) mostly the 17-bit one - both sources are confirmed by the data.

Evidence: nothing is fitted to the recordings except the one polarity bit. `T0` was found by a pooled search over the unknown phase (all 131,071 x 31 values) and then **confirmed independently**: the search's answer (T0 mod 131071 = 23618, T0 mod 31 = 11) singled out the cycle 678,973, which is exactly the `SKCTL := 3` write found in the traces (`analysis/raw-logs/sound-events.txt`, `dbg-pokey.txt`). With that T0 and no free parameter the rule predicts **110 of 110** start bits of the 55 independent ghost-eaten sweeps (three recordings: 32 + 14 + 9) and **87 of 87** sweeps including the repeat run; the audio correlation of the predicted assignment is 0.93-0.96 in every sweep (`analysis/evidence-ghost-sweep-*.txt`, section "Predicted start bits"). Chance would be 55 of 110.

Two corrections came with it: (1) `Timer.fires()` in `tools/pokey_timers.py` treated a write on the very cycle of a fire differently from `count()`/`pending()` (the fire reloaded with the NEW value); the dumps validate the second convention (the fire reloads with the OLD value, the write comes after it), so `fires()` was fixed (test added) - before the fix 3 of the 110 bits were mispredicted; (2) one case was left that the timer fix did not explain: a fire that falls on the same cycle as an AUDF write of its channel is **not** sampled as noise (the previous gated fire decides). That is one deciding case among three coincidences, so it is recorded as a hypothesis (OV-13c, `COINCIDENT_FIRE_SAMPLES` in `pokey_noise.py`); without it 109 of 110 are predicted.

**5. How the recordings are made reliably.** Altirra pauses the emulation when its window is not the active one (`"Pause when inactive" = 1`), so a recording silently stops as soon as another window (a terminal, another program) takes the focus: several recordings of this work stalled after 8-30 s for that reason, not because of the harness. `tools/record_tune.ps1` now uses a private copy of the settings with the option off (`external-tools/Altirra/Altirra_record.ini`, created on first use); your own Altirra settings are not touched. With it, other windows may be used and the audio muted (Windows volume, not Altirra's own) during a recording.

**6. The mixer, understood and measured (OV-13b, `tools/mixer_test.py`, `tools/start_state_test.py`).** In the sweep three channels play at volume 4-8 at the same time, and the real audio was 12-16% below the linear sum of the volumes. A single channel is exactly linear (volume 4 and 8 measured as 4.00 and 8 units), which hid the cause. Altirra's POKEY (`pokey.cpp`, option "Audio: Non-linear mixing", **on** in `Altirra.ini`) does not add volumes:
- the four volume bits have **uneven weights**, 0.12 / 0.26 / 0.56 / 1.12 (in 1/8.24 of the full output), so volume 1 is 0.857 and volume 2 is 1.857 of the nominal 1 and 2 units (bits 2 and 3 are exact);
- the summed level x of all channels goes through a **saturation curve**: linear up to x = 0.14, then `0.14 + (1 - exp(-2.85 (x - 0.14))) / 2.85`; one channel at volume 8 is x = 0.136, just below the knee (hence exactly linear), three channels at volume 8 are x = 0.41, well above it (-20% at the peak);
- the output is **AC-coupled** (time constant 2.60 ms, applied per 28-cycle sample) and then **clamped** to [-0.65, +0.5] of POKEY's full-scale output (only reached when all three channels play at volume 15).

*Controlled test* (`tools/mixer_test.py --make-cmd`, `tools/altirra_mixer_test.cmd`, `tools/record_tune.ps1 -Cmd altirra_mixer_test.cmd`, `analysis/mixer-test-recorded.wav`, `analysis/evidence-mixer-test.txt`): a debugger breakpoint at the title routine writes a 67-byte 6502 program and a table into emulated RAM and jumps to it at frame 900; the program disables interrupts and steps through 18 configurations on channels 1, 3, 4 (one silent, 17 combinations of pure tones at 264 / 329 / 395 Hz with volumes 1, 4, 7, 8, 15), 72 frames each, so nothing else touches a sound register (the ROM is never changed). The amplitude of every channel's fundamental is measured in the recording and divided by the same channel's amplitude alone at volume 8, which cancels the gain of the audio chain (the chain is flat: ch3/ch1 = 1.009, ch4/ch1 = 1.015). Over the 29 channel readings, model against measurement:

| model | mean error | RMS error | worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| linear sum of the volumes | +21% | 34% | +94% |
| uneven bit weights only | +18% | 32% | +91% |
| + saturation curve | +0.6% | 2.2% | 7.3% |
| + AC coupling and output clamp (Altirra's whole chain) | **0.0%** | **0.8%** | **3.1%** |

Examples (measured / model): two channels at volume 8 0.918-0.921 / 0.920; three at volume 8 0.81 / 0.81; one channel at volume 15 1.725 / 1.725 (it already saturates: linear would say 1.875); volume 1 0.107 / 0.107 (linear 0.125); all three at volume 15 0.964-0.977 / 0.978-0.980 (without the clamp 1.03). The worst remaining deviations are small amplitudes (volume 1 beside volume 15, a few %).

*In the game's sweep* the chain removes the bias: per-frame model / recording RMS 0.99 in both volume states (linear 1.19 and 1.11); per recording the median level ratio is 0.95-1.02. `tools/analyze_ghost_sweep.py` uses this chain by default (`--mixer linear --no-ac` for the old one). That table had been applied to the channels that are high; it belongs to the channels that are LOW (the output is inverting): with that the sweep correlation, which had been lower than for the plain linear sum, rises to 0.9988-0.9993 (OV-13d / OV-13e, section below; `--mixer invtable`, the default of the analyzers).

## The whole game checked in the waveform domain (OV-14)

The spectral checks of `tools/analyze_tail_sound.py` need a clear pitch peak in a 12 ms window. They could not resolve a weak voice under a louder one, a window with an onset, or a fast sweep (OV-14 a-c). Everything the game plays is a pure tone on channels 1-4 (`AUDC` `$A1`-`$A8`, idle `$00`; `AUDCTL` `$00`, or `$84` in the ghost-eaten sweep), and everything needed to render that sample by sample is verified: the fire times (point 1), the start state of a flip-flop (point 4), the mixer (point 6). `tools/analyze_full_waveform.py` renders a whole recording from the register writes alone and compares it with Altirra's real audio in 50 ms windows (both band-passed 100 Hz - 5 kHz, correlation at lag 0, the time anchor is the first fire of the start tune).

| recording | length | windows with sound | correlation >= 0.90 | single-channel windows >= 0.90 | RMS ratio model / recording |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| start tune + what follows | 172 s | 1,750 | **98.1%** (median 0.9988) | 99.3% of 1,529 | 0.959 |
| `sound-events` | 176 s | 3,117 | **97.6%** (median 0.9985) | 99.2% of 2,765 | 0.960 |
| `sound-level12` | 152 s | 2,732 | **97.3%** (median 0.9917) | 99.4% of 2,446 | 0.960 |
| `sound-hp` | 222 s | 4,050 | **97.2%** (median 0.9885) | 99.1% of 3,529 | 0.960 |

The RMS ratio is a constant 0.960 with a spread of 0.5% (5-95%: 0.956-0.964 in three of the four recordings, 0.946-0.975 in the sweep-heavy `sound-hp`), i.e. the level of every sound in the game is right to within the one calibration constant, over a whole 4-minute game. (The table above was measured with the mixer before OV-13e; with the table on the LOW channels, the default now, `sound-events` / `sound-level12` / `sound-hp` pass in 98.5 / 98.2 / 98.9% of the windows, with a median of 0.9991 / 0.9926 / 0.9903 and the RMS ratio spread 0.956-0.961; `evidence-fullwave-*-invtable.txt`.) Pitch, timing and level of the tones that the old method called "pitch not resolved" (117 tones in three recordings) are therefore confirmed in the waveform, not only their level.

What the comparison found on the way (each one was a real error of the model, found because a whole game is a much sharper test than 87 sweeps):
- **The CPU clock.** The model used 1,789,773 Hz; the NTSC machine runs at 315/88/2 MHz = 1,789,772.727 Hz. The relative error of 1.5e-7 is one audio sample (23 us) after 150 s: windows late in a recording lagged by 2-3 samples and failed at high pitch (421 of 996 windows of one sound class in `sound-hp`). With the exact clock 97% of all windows pass (83% before) and the sweep correlations rose from 0.928-0.962 to 0.951-0.964.
- **A fire on the very cycle of an AUDF write gives no edge** (OV-13c, now closed). Altirra's renderer gets no output edge from a fire that falls on the same cycle as a write that CHANGES `AUDF` (a rewrite of the same value does nothing: `if (mAUDF[n] != value)` in `pokey.cpp`); the timer keeps its schedule (the debugger dumps agree), but the flip-flop neither toggles nor samples noise. Evidence: (1) in the long channel-2 run frames 2487-3803 of `sound-events` the polarity flips in the middle of the run exactly at the only two coincident writes (frames 2562.0 and 3398.0); (2) it removes the last miss of the 110 sweep start bits (109 -> 110); (3) it is exact: widening it to a window around the write (-1..+1, -2..0, 0..+2, -2..+2 cycles) lowers the pass rate from 97.6% to 63-79%. The timer model has `Timer.edges()` / `Timer.edge_count()` for it (`tools/pokey_timers.py`).
- **The start state of a tone outside the sweep IS given by the rule of point 4 (OV-14d, closed).** It looked like chance (31 of 58 channel-2 runs, 9 of 17 channel-1 runs) because the renderer added the parity of all fires since reset to the start state; that parity belongs to the sweep model's constant, not to a state. A controlled test, `tools/start_state_test.py` (an injected program starts 224 tones per channel at cycles moving by 5 cycles; idle `AUDF` 0, 2, 13, 20; tone `AUDF` 13, and 114 / 60 / 28 on channel 2), showed that "wrong" was exactly that parity in every trial. With the state = noise bit XOR 1 (channels 3, 4) or XOR 0 (channels 1, 2: output = flip-flop XOR the high-pass flip-flop, which sits at 1), **896 of 896 starts are predicted**, and in the game 62 of 62 runs. The polarity of a run is no longer a free parameter; `analyze_full_waveform.py` still fits it per run and reports that the rule was the better one every time. The earlier hypothesis scans (poly offsets, j-th last fire, a flip-flop that never re-samples) were all at chance because the quantity they were compared with was wrong.

OV-14 a-c, as asked: (a) the weak-voice, onset and equal-pitch cases are all inside the windows above; single-channel tones are confirmed in 99.1-99.4% of 50 ms windows. (b) The bonus sweep (and the start tune's channel 1 under the channel-2 tone) is the weakest part: windows with channels 1 and 2 together pass at 76-91% (`BONUS_APPEAR` windows 61-81%, `START_PLAY` 67-88%); their level is right (ratio 0.96) and no lag of up to 8 samples recovers them, so the relative phase of the two channels is off inside them (OV-14e). (c) The fast sweeps of channel 2 (AUDF 13-23, 1.1-2.3 kHz, changing every frame) are no longer biased: they pass like every other single-channel window. The ghost-eaten sweep windows (channels 1, 3, 4) pass 90-92% at 50 ms (87 of 87 sweeps pass as wholes at a correlation of 0.95-0.96, point 3; 50 ms slices are noisier than a 1 s sweep). **Update (OV-14d/e closed):** the weak spot in (b) was the polarity error of the renderer, not a phase error between the channels; with the corrected start state 99.9-100% of all windows pass in all four recordings (`evidence-fullwave-*-ov14d.txt`), no window with channels 1 and 2 together is below 0.90, and the figures in the table above and in point (b) are the old ones.

## The high-pass pair under the microscope (OV-13d)

With the verified mixer chain the ghost-eaten sweep correlated about 0.009 worse than with a plain linear sum (0.9594 against 0.9685 in `sound-events`; with the finding below 0.9993). Was that the mixer, the resampling, or the model of the high-pass pair (channel 1 filtered by channel 3, `AUDCTL = $84`)? `tools/hp_test.py` answers with controlled tones: an injected 6502 program (as `tools/mixer_test.py`) holds states of channels 1, 3, 4 with `AUDCTL = $84` or `$00`, **the register writes are traced** (a final burst of writes to `$07FF` forces the log to be flushed), and the recording is aligned to the model by `anchor()`. Sets: `sweep` (the real frame states and ablations), `pairs` (channel 1 alone, 12 pitch pairs), `bis` (AUDF3 stepping through 114...122), `hold` and `dur` (a state held for 1-80 frames), `nohp` (two channels, no filter), `edges` (single-channel AUDF steps), `replay1` / `replay`, and others. Evidence: `analysis/evidence-hp-test-*.txt`.

**The result: the high-pass pair is exact; the residual was the polarity of the mixer's table (high versus low channels).**

*1. The pair.* Channel 1 alone under the filter, 12 pitch pairs (AUDF3 - AUDF1 = 0, +-2, +-4, +-8, at AUDF1 = 114, 72, 44, 200): correlation with the model **0.9983-0.9996** (median 0.9994); 15 consecutive AUDF3 steps (`bis`): 0.9977-0.9996. The pulse ends (the latch updates) lie on the model's channel-3 fires in every row: the offset against the nearest model fire is one constant (the chain's delay, within a tick of the whole-tick alignment) with a spread of 0.2 ticks or less - 11 rows of `pairs` (0.91-1.02 ticks), 14 of `bis` (0.76-1.04), 12 of `dur` (0.88-0.97), 8 of `hold` (0.04-0.06; `evidence-hp-test-phase-*.txt`). Holds of 1-80 frames, in any order, make no difference. The latch at channel 3's fire (+0.5 cycle) and channel 1's edge at its fire (+2) of Altirra's source are therefore right; moving the latch by (AUDF3 - AUDF1) ticks (the "finding" of the first analysis) lowers the hold correlations from 0.992-1.000 to 0.50-0.97. The scheduler's timers equal the model in 864 of 864 `.pokey` readings (`evidence-hp-test-timers.txt`; re-checked on the `pairs` run: channel 3 exact at every dump, all periods), and single channels are exact (0.9998). The resampling is not the problem either: `render_bl()` (alias-free synthesis of the 100 Hz - 5 kHz signal from the edge list) and the box average agree to 0.0004.

*2. The mix: the table is indexed by the channels that are LOW* (OV-13e, closed). With two squares and no filter (`nohp`) the waveform correlates best with the plain linear sum, not with Altirra's table on the high channels, although the amplitude of each channel is 0.920 of its single value, exactly what the table says. Further tests (`tools/hp_test.py --analyze-gainphase`, `--analyze-volsw`, `--analyze-mixers`):
- **Phase.** Channels 1 and 4, volume 8, the same period, relative phase rotated between the rows (`phase` set, from nearly in phase to nearly opposite): the pair has **0.921 x one channel at every phase** (spread 0.0%). An instantaneous curve on the high channels would give 1.00 when the highs never overlap and 0.84 when they always do (`evidence-hp-test-gain-phase.txt`).
- **Volume switching.** Channel 4 switched between 0 and 8 every 1-12 frames beside a steady channel 1: channel 1's amplitude follows the configuration within a row (`evidence-hp-test-gain-volsw.txt`).
- **Unequal volumes** (`uneq` set: 18 rows, e.g. 15+1, 15+4, 8+4, 15+15, 12+12): a model "linear shape x the gain the table gives each channel on average" (`cfggain`) fits the fitted scale to 0.0% over all rows, but the correlation still preferred a product term of the OPPOSITE sign to the table's (mixing the table into it with weight -1 gave 0.9998 in every row).
- That is the table with the roles of high and low exchanged: **level = - f(volumes of the channels that are LOW)**, f = Altirra's curve (`--mixer invtable`). The output stage is inverting and the table is indexed by the bits that pull the output DOWN; I had applied the curve to the channels that are high, which differs exactly where the curve is not linear. (A volume change of a channel that is high then changes nothing, which also removed a spurious step at the frame boundaries of the replay.)

The windows of the ghost-eaten sweeps (channels 1, 3, 4) now all pass (0 of 145 below 0.90); the RMS ratio of the whole game is 0.960 with a spread of 0.956-0.961 in all three recordings (the sweep-heavy one had 0.946-0.975). The fitted level scale of the sweeps is a constant 1.034-1.040 in every sweep of the four recordings, i.e. the one calibration constant of the chain, and nothing else is left in the sweeps. Not tested: the output clamp [-0.65, 0.5] with this sign (it does not bind in any of the sweeps: identical results with and without), three channels at volume 15.

**Two alignment traps that cost most of the work, now guarded against.** (1) Aligning a recording to the model with one periodic signal fixes the alignment only modulo its period: channel 1 (115 ticks) and channel 3 (119 ticks) differ by 4 ticks, so a wrong choice of whole periods looks exactly like a latch offset of (AUDF3 - AUDF1) ticks. (2) A search around the first-sound estimate that is too narrow: the true alignment of one recording was 17,135 ticks (0.27 s) away, outside a window of +-15,000 ticks, and the best candidate inside it fabricated phase offsets of 39-54 ticks that "depended on the value of AUDF3". `hp_test.anchor()` now scores a sample of the steps against the fires of **all** audible channels (and channel 3, the filter clock) over +-120,000 ticks, then refines on all steps; trains of different periods cannot all line up at a wrong shift (`tests/test_hp.py`: a synthetic recording whose first row is silent).

## Aids used for the session (`tools/altirra_sound_events.cmd`, all in emulated RAM/PC, the ROM untouched, no sound register written)

Pac-Man cannot die (`$A923 -> $A936`, as in the earlier sessions); in a 1,500-frame cycle: the dot counter `zp_34` is set to `$50`/`$A0` so the bonus item appears; while the item is on screen the hit test at `$A93B` is forced (Pac-Man is not steered onto it; the routine behind it, including the sound, is the game's); the power-pellet flags are refilled and Pac-Man is placed on a power pellet and then on two ghosts; player 1's score is set to 9,990 in one cycle; the level-complete flag `zp_26` is set in another. Input: one START press at the controller register, the joystick bot. ## Limits (also in `OPEN-VERIFICATION.md`)

- The ghost-eaten sweep is predicted from the register writes and the boot time `T0` of the machine alone (OV-13a, closed). The level of the multi-channel mix is explained (non-linear mixer, point 6, OV-13b closed). The high-pass pair is exact (OV-13d, closed); the mixer's table is indexed by the channels that are low (OV-13e, closed: sweeps 0.9988-0.9993). The whole game is checked in the waveform domain (OV-14); the level chain (DAC table, AC coupling constant `kTau` from the source) is closed too (OV-15: per-volume model / recording ratio 0.957-0.962; the absolute scale is Altirra's own, `Audio: Volume` 0.5 x 2.1 x UNIT, model / recording 0.995 with a continuous-time AC coupling and **0.999 with Altirra's two discrete high-pass stages**, the 0.5 gain located in `settings.cpp` / `ATAudioFilter::mScale` (OV-15a, OV-44 closed, C-30); `analyze_full_waveform.py --absolute-scale`); open there: nothing in the waveform domain (OV-14d and OV-14e are closed: 99.9-100% of all 50 ms windows at correlation >= 0.90).
- Some tones are classed "pitch not resolved" (level still checked): 40 in the sound-events recording, 53 in the level-12 recording, 24 in the first long recording. Causes: a weak voice under a louder one in a 12-13 ms window (the fit at the expected pitch is within 15% of the best fit), a window in which another channel starts from silence (its onset transient is not modelled), and a weak peak of a group of equal-pitch tones that largely cancel. OV-43 (closed, `OPEN-VERIFICATION.md` C-28): `tools/check_segments_waveform.py` judges them in the waveform domain - every segment correlates >= 0.90 with the model, and in all unresolved tones the recording prefers the true model to one with that tone 20 cents off (resolution about 10 cents); the tones inside ghost-eaten sweeps (49 unresolved, 70 flagged items) are shift-tested too, with the sweep renderer (C-29: 390/390 unresolved and 83/83 flagged items at 20 cents).
- Only pure tones appear in the whole game (`AUDC` `$A_`); `AUDCTL` is 0 except during the ghost-eaten sweep ($84). Other `AUDCTL` bits or distortions would be reported by the analyzer as not modelled; none occurred.
- PAL timing (`/pal`: 312 lines, 1,773,447 Hz) and Altirra's built-in OS (`/kernel:hle`) were run with the same analyzers (OV-42, `OPEN-VERIFICATION.md` C-24): PAL 4,247 of 4,247 windows >= 0.90, HLE 4,067 of 4,067 (after OV-42a: a sweep that begins inside a channel-1 tone takes channel 1's state from its own run, C-25). With the PAL OS ROM (MAME `a800pal`) 4,345 of 4,345 windows (after OV-42c: a fire on the cycle of a write that changes `AUDCTL` gives no edge, like one on a changing `AUDF` write, C-26). Not tested: real hardware (OV-42b, closed as a documented limit by the owner's decision, C-27: not a condition for the 100 % decompilation); the emulator's audio path, including its AC coupling and resampler, is part of what is measured.
- The start tune at later start levels is now run (StartLevel 4 and 12 through the real menu, and every poked level): see the section on the level.
