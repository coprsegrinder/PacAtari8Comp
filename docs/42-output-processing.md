# 42 - Output processing (stage 64)

GENERATED by `python -I tools/analyze_output.py --write analysis/evidence-output-summary.txt --doc docs/42-output-processing.md` from the write map of `tools/output_map.py`, the display lists decoded from the ROM and the curated text of the tool; tests: `tests/test_output.py`. The inventory of the chips the game writes is `33` (stage 57) and `35` (stage 59: every write to ANTIC, GTIA and POKEY traced); the sound is `22`; the colours `15`; the pictures `16`, `20`; the screens `19`, `21`. This chapter is the **memory side**: where the game's pictures and text live, which instruction writes which region, and how a frame turns the state into outputs.

## 1. The result

- **The game has five kinds of output** (section 2): the **screen** (character memory the display list shows: the maze band, the status rows, the title), the **player-missile pages** (Pac-Man, the four ghosts, the pop-up score), the **character sets** (written once by `Start`), the **colours and positions** in the chip registers (and their OS shadows), and the **sound** (POKEY). Nothing else leaves the program.
- **Every store of the ROM is classified by the regions it can write** (section 4): of the 574 store instructions of the ROM, 188 can write an output region, and each of the 16 output regions has a short, named list of writer routines.
- **The classification was checked on the machine**: the real code run on the concrete 6502 over 12000 frames of three kinds of set-up, rare paths, 2000 display-list interrupts and the start-up expansion made **2,303,680 stores, none outside its instruction's classification**; 180 of the 188 output stores ran (the others are direct stores of the end of the eaten-ghost pause and the key loop, exact by their address and seen live in `33`).
- **Two strays of the original** are in the map and in the port's way if it copies the code instead of the behaviour (section 6): `InitDisplay2` ends with a jump into the ghost-drawing loop with X = `$81`, so it writes 16 bytes of the **cartridge ROM** page `$A5` (no effect); and the first display-list interrupt, when the colour of band 1 is 0 (the attract-mode fade), writes one byte to `$D111` (no hardware there).

Reading the table as a pipeline: **the state** (positions, directions, states, timers, counters: `25`) is turned into **pictures** by `StepAndDrawEntity` (a ghost: 16 rows from `$BAC0 + shape` into the buffer `$0410`, then into its page at its row, `HPOSP` := X), `DrawAllInPlace` (all four ghosts and Pac-Man without moving them: the board start, the level end, the death), `GhostEaten` (the pop-up), `DeathAnimation` (Pac-Man's last picture rows), and into **text and cells** by `AddToScore` (the score digits), `DrawLives`, `DrawLevelIndicator`, `EatDotAtPacMan`, `BlinkPowerPellets` / `ShowPowerPellets`, `BonusItemUpdate`, `ClearReadyText`, `TurnControl` (the READY! and PLAYER ONE / TWO text). The routine that draws is also the one that moves: **drawing is the last step of an entity's update**, so the screen of a frame is the state after that frame's update.

## 5. The sound

Sound is the one output with no memory form: 31 sound-register writes in 11 routines drive the four POKEY channels, and `22` has each sound with its numbers and its three layers of checking (the registers, the emulator's waveform, the recorded wave). The summary the port needs: **channel 1** the dot sweep, the start tune (with channel 2), the bonus item, the death glide, the ghost-eaten sweep (with channels 3 and 4, `AUDCTL` = `$84`); **channel 2** the siren, the frightened wobble and the eyes sound, with the priority eyes > frightened > siren and a mute for the 34 frames of a ghost eaten; every other channel is silent (`ClearPokeyRegisters`). All sounds are driven by the frame counter and the game state (`24`): the sound is a function of the state and the frame, never of the input directly.

## 6. Two strays of the original

- **`InitDisplay2` writes 16 bytes of ROM.** The routine (`$A610`) clears the player and sound registers, installs the title display list and colours, and then ends with `jmp $B985`, the shared tail of the ghost-drawing loop; at that point X = `$81`, so the tail forms `DstPtr` = `$A5` + the old low byte and copies 16 rows of whatever `SrcPtr` points to into the cartridge: the writes are ignored by the hardware. Live: the 881 calls of a 20-second title session all had X = `$81`, `DstPtrH` = `$A5` (`analysis/raw-logs/id2.txt`); the models make the 35,856 stores of the map to the ROM page. The page `$A500-$A50F` read at every frame entry of that session (`analysis/raw-logs/romstray.txt`) equals the ROM file in every frame: the writes change nothing. A port must **not** reproduce it.
- **Band 1's colour of 0 clears the players' colours through a wrong index.** In `DliHandler1` the second band loads `DliColors[0]` into X and branches on the flags of that load: when the colour is 0 (the attract-mode fade of `$3A`: `($3A xor COLRSH) and DRKMSK`) the branch falls through into the loop that zeroes `COLPM3..0` with X = 0, `dex` makes it `$FF`, and the store `sta COLPM0,x` goes to `$D111` (no hardware), once. The effect is none; the intended effect (the last band clears the player colours) does not happen in that band.

## 7. Verification

- **The classification** (`tools/output_map.py`): 574 store instructions, 188 with an output target; run on the concrete 6502 (`tools/cpu6502.py`): 12000 handler frames (the game, play and title set-ups of `tools/algorithm_models7.py` with the entities placed where the maze lets them stand and the maze pointer inside the maze), the level-end stages, the eaten-ghost pause, the death animation, `EatDotAtPacMan` and `BonusItemUpdate` on random cells, 2000 display-list interrupts, the start-up expansion: **2,303,680 stores, 0 outside the classification of their instruction**.
- **Not run by these models** (8 of 188 output stores, all direct stores, classified exactly by their address): `$A4A0` (Start), `$A4E6` (Start), `$A4F7` (Start), `$B088` (GhostEatenPause), `$B093` (GhostEatenPause), `$B098` (GhostEatenPause), `$B0AA` (GhostEatenPause), `$B0AD` (GhostEatenPause). The key loop's stores to `CH` and the restart's `GPRIOR` are in `38` / `40` and `35`; the end of the eaten-ghost pause is run live in `18`.
- **Every chip register write** is a static site and was traced live (`35`: 4.66 million writes, 0 outside the 71 sites); **every I/O instruction ran live** (`33`: 133 of 133). The screen memory and the player-missile pages were compared with the models over 30,000 frames with the whole memory compared (`31` section 5); the colours of 482,764 pixels (`15`), the nine pictures of Pac-Man and the ghost shapes against screenshots (`16`, `20`).

## 8. For the port

- **A renderer needs, per frame: the maze band** (23 x 40 cells of glyph codes, `MazeScreen`), **two status rows** (80 cells), **five movable objects** (four ghosts, Pac-Man) with their 16 picture rows, positions, colours and priority, **the pop-up and the death pictures**, **the colour bands** (five a frame) and **the title screen**; the data are in `12` (glyphs, shapes, text) and the state that selects them in `25` / `30`.
- **The order** of a frame's output is the order of the update: Pac-Man, the dot, the ghosts from 3 down to 0, each drawn at the end of its update (`24` section 4.3); a port that draws once at the end of the frame gets the same picture.
- **Do not copy the strays** of section 6; do copy the **cell codes** and the **row-by-row colour bands**, which are visible.
- **The 8-bit shifts**: the game moves a ghost by 1 pixel and an entity's picture by whole rows; `HPOSP` takes the X in colour clocks (2 per pixel of mode 4), the Y is the row of the first picture line in the page: `16`, `20`.

## 9. Limits

- The classification of the 14 `sta (zp),y` instructions is read from the pointer analysis of `29` and confirmed by the run; the other stores are classified by address, which is exact.
- The concrete runs use the random frame set-ups of the stage-55 models, constrained to positions the maze allows; a state outside what a game can reach (a GameState of 5, an entity at Y = `$FF`, a pointer into the original maze) does write elsewhere, which is why the models constrain them (section 7).
- Nothing here is pixel-exact: the pixels are ANTIC's and GTIA's, and were checked against Altirra's screen in `15`, `16` and `20` (Altirra 4.40, NTSC and PAL); a real Atari was not available (OV-42b).
