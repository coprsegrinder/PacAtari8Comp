The transition rules of the state variables are `34` (stage 58: 68 rules proved against the code); the two players are `19`; the levels `14`; the ghost states `17`, `18`. This chapter is the **state itself**: which bytes are state, which are scratch, what each event does to them, and what a port has to keep.

## 1. The result

- **The state of a running game is 144 zero-page bytes** (section 2) **plus the maze, the saved mazes and the score cells**: the experiment changed each of the 256 bytes in a running game, and for 144 of them the outputs or the state a few frames later differ; the other 112 are scratch, OS shadows, constants of the interrupts, or unused. The catalogue of `25` / `30` is right about the structures: of the bytes it calls board or game, 128 of 132 influence the future (all the game bytes), and every one of its temporaries does not (after the corrections of section 3).
- **The disagreements with `30` are findings** (section 3): the six `ScoreAdd` digits are not temporaries but **zero-at-rest** state (the callers set only one digit and `AddToScore` adds all six); `WantVert`, `WantHoriz` and `PacHold` are temporaries of one call, not board state; and the bytes only the display-list interrupts, the key loop and `Start` read do not show in a frame experiment.
- **Each state-management event has a measured effect** (section 4): `InitBoard` resets 114 bytes to constants (every board and every turn), the restart of a game 144, `NewBoardInit` and `ClearPlayerCounters` only the player's counters, a turn hand-over only the stage of the switch; the table gives every case.
- **The two players are two copies of seven variables** (section 5): the experiment finds exactly `Lives`, `ExtraLifeFlag`, `Level`, `PelletFlags`, `DotsEaten` (lo, hi) and `BonusCount` of the player **on turn** as state, and the other player's seven as no influence at all while the first plays; the other player's maze lives in `SavedMaze1/2` and nothing else is swapped.

## 2. The state vector

**The experiment** (`tools/state_probe.py`): a game state (a frame of play for player 1, for player 2, a title frame, or one of the frame classes of `38` with its dispatcher variables, entities on junction cells, ghosts in the documented states, Pac-Man on a dot half of the time) is copied; one byte of the zero page is changed in the copy (to 0, a power of two, `$FF`, its complement, its successor, the value of another byte, Pac-Man's X or Y); both copies run the real frame handler for 20 frames with the same random numbers and the same clock; every store to an output region and the state at the end (the zero page except the changed byte, the maze screen, the status rows) are compared. A byte for which any trial shows a difference **influences the future**. 80 trials a byte and mode; a second run of 400 trials for the 23 bytes that the first run left doubtful (5 more showed up as state).

Where they differ (the byte lists are in `analysis/evidence-state-summary.txt`):

- **ScoreAdd (`$94-$99`)**: docs/30 said 'temporary (set before use)'; the experiment says STATE: `AddToScore` adds all six digits and clears them, and the callers set only the digit they need (`$98` for a dot), so **the other five must be zero between uses** - a zero-at-rest invariant, not a temporary (docs/30 now says so).
- **WantVert / WantHoriz (`$D6-$DD`), PacHold (`$A7`)**: docs/30 said board; experiment: no influence - `SteerToTarget` writes the wanted directions before it reads them, `$A7` is set every frame before `MovePacMan`: **temporaries of one call or one frame** (docs/30 now says so).
- **EyesReturning (`$F2`), DeathFreeze (`$EE`)**: no influence in 400 trials: `$F2` is recomputed from the ghost states every frame (`EyesAndHousePerFrame`), `$EE` is set when the death starts.
- **OS and the interrupts' inputs (`$10`, `$18`, `$4D-$4F`, `$5E-$61`, `$A0`)**: read by the display-list interrupts, `Start` or the OS, not by the frame handler the experiment runs: **state of the machine, not of the frame** (the colours, the attract timer).
- **The key loop's bytes (`$1E`, `$45`, `$46`)**: read by the main program (`38`, `40`), not by the handler.
- **Rare in the sample (`$9E`, `$C1`, `$C2`, `$C5`, `$C8`, `$D4`, `$D5`, `$5A-$5D`)**: docs/30 and the experiment agree once the trials are enough: 1 to 35 of 400 trials show a difference (these bytes matter only in some ghost states or on some frames).

## 5. Two players

The experiment ran the same game once with player 1 on turn and once with player 2. The bytes that influence the future **only for the player on turn**:

| Player on turn | Bytes | Variables |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $2C, $2E, $30, $32, $34, $36, $43 | `Lives[0]`, `ExtraLifeFlag[0]`, `Level[0]`, `PelletFlags[0]`, `DotsEatenLo[0]`, `DotsEatenHi[0]`, `BonusCount[0]` |
| 2 | $2D, $2F, $31, $33, $35, $37, $44 | `Lives[1]`, `ExtraLifeFlag[1]`, `Level[1]`, `PelletFlags[1]`, `DotsEatenLo[1]`, `DotsEatenHi[1]`, `BonusCount[1]` |

These are `Lives`, `ExtraLifeFlag`, `Level`, `PelletFlags`, `DotsEatenLo`, `DotsEatenHi` and `BonusCount`: the `Player[2]` structure of `30`, found by experiment. **No other zero-page byte is per player**: Pac-Man's position, the ghosts and every timer belong to the board in play, and `InitBoard` clears or refills them at each turn (section 4), so nothing is carried from one player's turn to the other's. What the turn hand-over moves is the **maze**: the leaving player's maze goes to `SavedMaze1` or `SavedMaze2` (`Copy0C00To1000` / `Copy0C00To1400`) and the arriving player's maze comes back to `$0C00` (`$AA94-$AACC`), with the labels 1UP / 2UP redrawn and `CurrentPlayer` toggled; the score digits stay where they are, in the two sets of cells of the status rows.

## 6. The state machines

The top-level machine is `GameState` (`$19`): 1 (copyright set up), 2 (copyright shown), 3 (title menu), 4 (title after a game), 0 (a game or the game-over countdown); START leaves any of them for 0 through the restart, any other console key goes from 0, 1 or 2 to 3 (`41` section 4). **In a game** the machine is the one of `38` (15 frame classes, 25 transitions seen) over these state variables, each with its values and its writers in `34`: `IntroRequest`, `PlayStarted`, `StartTuneActive` (the board start and the start tune), `WaitState` (the READY! wait and the turn handling), `TurnSwitchState`, `LevelComplete` with `LevelEndStep` (the level end), `DeathState` with `PacFrozen`, `DeathAnimActive` (the death), `EatPause` (the eaten-ghost pause), `Paused`, `CountdownActive`, `FrightenPhase` and the four `GhostState`s (the ghost machines of `17`, `18`). The 68 rules of `34` say which write is allowed in which state; this chapter's experiment says which bytes carry the state between frames.

## 7. For the port

- **A save state** is the 144 state bytes of section 2 (their addresses are listed), the maze screen (920 bytes), the other player's maze (1,024) if two players, and the score and high-score cells (18 bytes: `$202A-$202F`, `$2039-$203E`, `$2047-$204C`); the picture buffers, the player-missile pages and the colour bands are derived and redrawn. A port may hold the same data in structures (`30` section 7 has the proposal) as long as it keeps the **invariants** the code relies on: the six `ScoreAdd` digits are zero between uses; `MazePtr` (`$80-$81`) points at Pac-Man's cell of the maze screen; `zp_83` / `zp_84` hold the last cell a dot was tested at; `PacDirection` is 1, 2, 4 or 8 (or 0 before the first move) and the wanted direction a subset of the four bits (`41`).
- **Reset at the right moments**: every turn and every board calls `InitBoard` (the 114 constants of section 4: the start positions, the timers, the ghost states, the flags); a new game adds the restart's 144 (the lives, the level, the counters, the maze copies); a new level adds `AdvanceLevel`'s. A port that rebuilds a struct at those moments and keeps the rest across frames reproduces the game's state management.
- **Scratch is free**: the 112 bytes that do not influence the future may be locals; but `ScoreAdd` is not scratch.

## 8. Limits

- The experiment is a sensitivity analysis: a byte is called state when a trial shows a difference, so the **state is a lower bound** (a byte that matters only in a rare state may be missed: the doubtful ones were re-run with 400 trials, and 5 of 23 more showed up); a byte that never showed a difference in thousands of trials with the entities, ghosts and dispatcher in the documented states is scratch in practice.
- Only the frame handler is run: the bytes read only by the display-list interrupts, the key loop and `Start` (the colours, the attract timer, the key debounce, the countdown timer's reader) are not seen as state, though they are state of the machine; section 3 lists them.
- The events are run from random states of the stage-55 set-ups with the entities placed where the maze lets them stand; a **restart** from the title (GameState 3 or 4) skips `InitBoard` at `$A48D` and is not the case measured (START in play and from the title menu both come through `$A537`, which sets GameState 0 first).
- Altirra 4.40 was not needed for this chapter: the experiments are on the concrete 6502; the live sessions of `34` and `38` are the check that the states they visit are the game's.
