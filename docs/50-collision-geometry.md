# 50 - The collision geometry (OV-68)

GENERATED by `python -I tools/analyze_collision_doc.py --doc docs/50-collision-geometry.md` from `tools/gtia_collisions.py`, the evidence of the two live audits and the curated text of the tool; tests: `tests/test_collide.py`. The game's contact test reads three registers of the GTIA (`M1PL`, `M2PL`, `M2PF`); they are computed by the hardware from the pictures of the players and missiles and the playfield, so a port has to compute them itself (`45`, `48` section 8). This chapter is that computation, **checked against the real GTIA of the real game**.

## 1. The result

- **The model equals the hardware**: `collision_registers(positions, missile page, player pages, playfield)` returns the sixteen registers `$D000-$D00F` of a scene. On **73,111 random scenes (44,064 distinct)** put on the screen of the real game by a stub (six runs: the maze, a test screen with every character code, positions and rows anywhere) all sixteen registers equal the GTIA's: **0 differences**; on **27,193 frames of real play** (the scene of the frame before and the registers the game reads, in sessions with contacts, 9,075 of them with the latch carried over from earlier frames) **0 differences**; the 17,982 frames that follow a handler still running when the beam reached the objects are set aside and 2 of them differ (section 4).
- **The three registers the game reads** are the contact test (`M1PL` and `M2PL`: Pac-Man's second and third missile touch the player of ghost g, then `|dy| < 5`, `41`) and the bonus pick-up (`M2PF` bit 1: missile 2 touches playfield colour 1, the bonus item). In real play: M1PL 12784 (bits: 8389 4943 1512 1222), M2PL 12486 (8156 4760 1495 1166), M2PF 5544 (3064 0 3028 0); **`PF1` (bit 1) never occurred in those sessions** (no bonus item was picked up), so the mapping of the pixel value 2 to `PF1` and of value 3 with bit 7 to `PF3` rests on the test-screen runs (every character code under the objects: 2,000 to 11,000 scenes with each of those bits set).
- **The rules are few and exact** (section 3): a player is 8 pixels, a missile 2, one pixel one colour clock; the playfield is read in cells of 4 clocks by 8 lines; a bit is set for any coinciding pixel **on clocks 34-221 and lines 8-247 only**; the registers latch until `HITCLR`. **Things the audit found** that the first model had wrong: the maze's first row is at player-missile row 40 (not 32); objects at HPOS 0 (off the visible area) collide with nothing; rows from 248 on collide with nothing; and **the registers keep their latched overlaps through the death and eaten-ghost frames** (no `HITCLR`).
- **For the port**: `tools/platform_iface.py` `GtiaCollisions(scene)` is a `Platform` whose `collisions(w)` is this model. What the model takes is the **scene of the frame before** (section 5). **Producing that scene from the game state is the drawing**, which is in port form since OV-69 (`core_draw`, checked against the ROM, `44`): `core_draw.scene(w, charset)` hands the World's scene to the model.

## 2. What the game reads, and when

The registers **latch** every overlap shown **since the last `HITCLR`** (`$D01E`). The game writes `HITCLR` in three places: once per frame in the contact step (`$A98C`, right after the contact test and before it moves and draws anything), in `InitBoard` (`$ADED`) and at the end of the eaten-ghost pause (`$B0AD`). So in a normal frame the registers read at the start of handler k+1 hold the overlaps of **the display of frame k**, the scene handler k left in the player-missile pages and the position registers; but in a frame in which the game does **not** reach a `HITCLR` (the death sequence, the eaten-ghost pause) the latch keeps what it had and adds what the frame shows - the audit found this in the death frames, where the ghosts' positions are set to 0 and the latched contact stays. A port keeps the latch: `GtiaCollisions.collisions(w)` is read at the start of the frame, `frame_done(w)` clears it if the frame wrote `HITCLR` and then adds the overlaps of the scene the frame left on the screen.

## 3. The geometry

*Positions in colour clocks (HPOS), scan lines as the player-missile pages count them (page row = scan line; the picture starts at line 8). `SIZEP` and `SIZEM` are 0 in the game.*

| Object | Picture | Where its pixels are |
|---|---|---|
| player p (the four ghosts) | one byte a row in page `$2400 + 256 p`, bit 7 leftmost | bit b at clock `HPOSP[p] + 7 - b` |
| missile m (Pac-Man's four missiles) | two bits a row in the missile page `$2300`: missile m owns bits `2m+1` (left) and `2m` | clocks `HPOSM[m]` and `HPOSM[m] + 1` |
| the playfield (the maze band) | 23 rows of 40 character cells at `$0C00`, ANTIC mode 4, character set `$3000` | cell (row, col): lines `40 + 8 row ...`, clocks `48 + 4 col ...`; each row of the glyph is four pixels of two bits, one pixel one clock; the value 0 is the background (no collision), 1, 2, 3 are `PF0`, `PF1`, `PF2` (`PF3` when the character code has bit 7 set and the value is 3) |

The register bits (all verified on the hardware):

| Registers | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `M0PF`-`M3PF` (`$D000-$D003`) | bit k: missile n touched playfield colour k |
| `P0PF`-`P3PF` (`$D004-$D007`) | bit k: player n touched playfield colour k |
| `M0PL`-`M3PL` (`$D008-$D00B`) | bit k: missile n touched player k (its own number too) |
| `P0PL`-`P3PL` (`$D00C-$D00F`) | bit k: player n touched player k (never its own number) |

A bit is set for **any scan line and clock** at which the two pixels coincide; priority, colours and the 5th-player bit of `PRIOR` do not matter (the game runs with `GPRIOR` `$11`: the audits ran in that state).

## 4. How it was checked

**A. Random scenes on the real GTIA** (`tools/collide_stub.s`, `tools/make_collide_cmd.py`, `tools/altirra_collide.cmd`, `tools/analyze_collide.py`). A 425-byte stub in free RAM of the emulated machine replaces the frame body from the first frame of play (test aid; the display-list interrupts, the OS and the screen go on): every third frame it reads the sixteen collision registers **as the CPU reads them**, clears them (`HITCLR`), and puts a new random scene on the screen: eight positions near each other (so that overlaps are frequent) and 16 rows for each of the five objects with one of four densities of set bits, over the real maze. The scene and the registers are dumped with the debugger. The registers of a record belong to the **previous** scene, shown for three frames.

- **Integrity**: the stub's random generator is deterministic; `analyze_collide.py` runs the same stub on the concrete 6502 from the run's seed and compares **every record's scene** with what it makes: 6 of 79,731 records differ (the first record of each log, made before the first scene was installed): the records are not corrupted by anything the emulated machine did. (A first version of the stub, which took more than a frame, was re-entered by the next frame's handler and wrote corrupted scenes: this check found it; the stub now runs every third frame.)
- **The comparison**: runs `collide-s1.txt` (12625 records), `collide-s2.txt` (13395 records), `collide-s3.txt` (13422 records), `collide-s4.txt` (13428 records), `collide-s5.txt` (13422 records), `collide-s6.txt` (13439 records); **73,111 scenes compared, 0 differences** in all sixteen registers.
- **Coverage** (the table at the end of `analysis/evidence-collide-summary.txt`): all sixteen registers, every bit that can be set (the four playfield colours included, thanks to the test screen), were seen both set and clear in thousands of scenes.
- **What the audit found out**: (1) the first model put the maze's first row at line 32; the hardware showed **40** (found by trying all offsets from -8 to +8 on 150 scenes: exactly one gives 0 differences). (2) Wide scenes (positions anywhere) fitted the visible clocks: **34 to 221** gives 0 differences on 2,500 scenes, every neighbouring pair 19 or more. (3) Tall scenes (rows anywhere): the last line is **247** (246 and 248 give 3 differences each). The player-missile geometry (the clock of each pixel, which bit is which missile pixel) was right at the first try. (4) **A flaw of the audit itself, found and fixed**: the stub's random generator repeated after 512 scenes, so the first runs compared only about 500 distinct scenes; the install counter is now mixed into the seed and all six runs were redone (the distinct-scene count is in the result).

**B. The real game in play** (`tools/run_collide_play_audit.ps1`, sessions `death`, `catch`, `frightened_cases`): at the entry of the frame handler of every frame the debugger dumps the GTIA positions, the pages `$2300-$27FF`, the maze screen `$0C00-$0F97` and `$D000-$D00F`; and logs the executions of `sta HITCLR` and the end of the handler with their scan line; `analyze_collide.py --play` evaluates the model with the latch rule (`A[i] = (0 if a HITCLR ran in handler i-1, else A[i-1]) | model(scene i)`, the chain carried by the model, not by the hardware's earlier values). **27,193 frames compared, 0 differences, 9,075 of them carrying the latch over** (the death sequence and the eaten-ghost pause are such stretches). Nothing is written to the emulated machine.

**Frames that cannot be judged this way**: when the handler is still running as the beam reaches the objects (its end, or a `HITCLR`, falls on scan line 40 or later of the next frame) it draws and clears **in the middle of the display**, and the registers hold the collisions of the old scene for the lines before that moment. 17,982 frames are of this kind in these sessions (long frames: eyes returning, the intro, level changes); the model is not claimed for them, but it was evaluated: **2 of the 17,982 differ**. A port whose frames ran in zero time would see the full-frame scene; an exact copy of the mixture the original sees would need the handler's cycle timing: a limit of this model, section 6.

## 5. For the port

```python
from platform_iface import GtiaCollisions
platform = GtiaCollisions(scene_of_the_previous_frame)     # scene(w) -> (hpos[8], missile_page, player_pages, Playfield(cells, charset))
```

`play_frame` takes the registers as the input `w.collide`; the model gives them. What it needs is the scene: the eight position values the game writes (`EntityX` for the ghosts; Pac-Man's four missiles), the pictures in the pages, the maze cells (`w.maze`) and the game character set (the asset pack, `47`). The pictures and positions are what the original's **drawing routines** write (`42`: `StepAndDrawEntity`, `DrawAllInPlace`, `ClipEntityAtTunnel`, `GhostEaten`, `DeathAnimation`, the Pac-Man draw in `MovePacMan`): **those are in port form** (`tools/core_draw.py`, OV-69) and write `w.pm`, `w.hpos` and `w.picbuf`; each equals the ROM's pages, positions and buffers (`tools/check_draw.py` and every frame of `check_play`, `44`). The chain is therefore: the port's scene equals the ROM's, and the model on the ROM's scene equals the hardware's registers (sections 3 and 4): the port's registers equal the hardware's. `core_draw.scene(w, charset)` and `GtiaCollisions` connect them.

## 6. Limits

- **Scene space**: the maze-band runs put objects at rows 48 to 207 and clocks 72 to 168, the wide and tall runs anywhere (the playfield registers only where all five objects are inside the maze band, the player and missile registers from row 32 on: above that the stub's own install is still running as the beam passes). **Playfield collisions outside the maze band are not modelled**: the status rows (hires text) and the area below the maze produced differences in the tall run and are excluded; the game's ghosts and Pac-Man never stand there (the top junction row is at line 44). The registers of objects in the first lines (8 to 31) are unaudited.
- **The handler's timing** (section 4): frames in which the handler outlasts the start of the objects' lines show a mixture of two scenes; the model gives the full-frame answer. The game's measured timing: the handler never overruns a frame, a play frame takes a median of 37 lines (`38`).
- **Sizes and delays**: `SIZEP` / `SIZEM` are 0 in the game; `VDELAY` is 0; the model does only that.
- **One configuration**: `GPRIOR` `$11`, `GRACTL` `$03`, `CHBASE` `$30` in the maze band; the pop-up frames (`GPRIOR` `$14`, eaten-ghost pause) are not audited - the contact test does not run then.
- **The playfield of the model** is the maze band of the game screen; colours of the playfield (`COLPF`) are irrelevant to collisions.
- **The scene production** (the drawing of the sprites) is in port form (OV-69, closed); the drawing of the maze cells and the text is not needed here: the collision model reads the cells.
